# [HR-SOP-00] HR procedures: how to use them

Tags: SOP, Draft

**Next review:** October 2027

## Purpose

This is the starting point for LeisureWorld's everyday HR procedures: absence, sickness, lateness and leave. Each procedure is short and step by step. It says who does what, by when, and what gets recorded in Turnfin.

Applies to: every supervisor, duty manager, centre manager and the operations manager, at every LeisureWorld centre.

## The procedures

- **HR-SOP-01 Reporting an absence**: every member of staff.
- **HR-SOP-02 Sickness absence on the day**: duty manager or supervisor on shift.
- **HR-SOP-03 Keeping in touch and the return-to-work talk**: line manager.
- **HR-SOP-04 Repeated or patterned absence**: line manager, centre manager.
- **HR-SOP-05 Long-term sickness absence**: line manager, operations manager.
- **HR-SOP-06 Lateness and absence without permission**: duty manager, line manager.
- **HR-SOP-07 Annual leave requests and refusals**: line manager.
- **HR-SOP-08 Force majeure, compassionate and other leave**: line manager, operations manager.
- **HR-SOP-09 Absence records and medical privacy**: everyone who records absence.

## Who is who in these procedures

- **The staff member**: the person who is off, late or asking for leave.
- **The person on shift in charge**: the duty manager, or the supervisor running the rota for that department today. In Turnfin they hold **Rota: Run**.
- **The line manager**: the person's manager on their HR record in Turnfin.
- **The centre manager** and **the operations manager**: for formal steps, long-term cases and anything unusual.

## Gaps to fill in

Where a procedure says **Set by LeisureWorld**, the detail (a phone number, a time, a number of days) comes from the staff handbook or the contract. Fill each one in before this procedure is published, and keep it the same as the handbook.

## The rules every procedure follows

- Be fair and consistent: treat the same situation the same way for everyone.
- Never write medical details in Turnfin. Record only that someone is off and the reason (sickness, family emergency, bereavement or other).
- Talk to the person first. Most absence is handled by a short, kind conversation.
- If something might be linked to a disability, pregnancy, or a problem at work, stop and speak to the operations manager before taking any formal step.
- If the law and a procedure ever disagree, the law wins. Ask the operations manager.
